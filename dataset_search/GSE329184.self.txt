!Series_summary = Senescence is a key pathomechanism across chronic diseases but highly heterogeneous. In this study we demonstrate that high-content imaging and transcriptomics can jointly shed light on senescence in vitro, distinguishing healthy and chemically induced senescence, differentiation from quiescence and characterization of senolytic response.
!Series_overall_design = Dermal (NHDF) and lung (NHLF) fibroblasts from two donors respectively are used for senescence study adn are passaged 5 to 7 times. Late passage cells at passage 25 or above are used as healthy senescence control. Tool compounds were used to induce quiescence or senescence. The compounds are 10 μM Rapamycin (Sigma, 553211), 5 – 20 μg/mL Bleomycin (Sigma, b7216), 50ng/mL Nocodazole (Sigma, sml1665), 4.5 μM Palbociclib (Cell Signaling Technology, 47284). Vehicle control with DMSO at 0.2% to mimic vehicle concentration of Palbociclib. Tool compounds were added 24-hour post seeding. For cells treated with rapamycin, nocodazole, and bleomycin, media were refreshed after 48 hours of treatment. For palbociclib and vehicle control, no media change performed until end of experiment at day 7. On day 5, cells were added with either media alone, or with 0.1 μM or 1 μM Navitoclax to study the cell response to senolytic compound with or without senescence inducers. The processed file only includes 230 samples used as part of the study, while raw data is provided for 240 samples. From the missing samples, samples 0235 and 0010 were excluded during sequencing QC. The others were excluded in our study focusing on multi-modal integration due to experimental concerns on the separate CellPainting plate with matched experimental plate layout. The quality of the transcriptomics data itself was not assessed.
